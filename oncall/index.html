On call tonight · three incidents

On call tonight

The pager goes off. What do you check first?

You will learn: how to work an incident from symptom to cause, one decision at a time, on three of the most common database pages.

Each incident starts with an alert. At every step you pick what to do, and you are told whether it was a good call, a workable one, or one that would make things worse, and why. The outputs are written to look like the real commands, and they are samples, not captured runs. The causes are the ones the rest of this site measures and explains.

For the real thing, the first five minutes checklist and the on-call reading path are the pages to keep open.

The one invariant: COMMIT means the log record is on disk. pg_wal in PostgreSQL, #innodb_redo/#ib_redo0 + mysql-bin in MySQL, journal/WiredTigerLog + the oplog in MongoDB. The data files (base/…, *.ibd, collection-*.wt) are where checkpoints place the result afterwards, on their own schedule. Every guarantee on this page, durability, replication, point-in-time recovery, is a consequence of that ordering, and every number on this page is checkable with the commands given beside it.

PostgreSQL, MySQL/InnoDB, and MongoDB/WiredTiger internals, from physical layout to production failure modes. File names, paths, thresholds, and commands are the production ones.
