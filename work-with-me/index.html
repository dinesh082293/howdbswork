The health check

A written review of one database, ranked by risk and by cost

I read the same things this site teaches you to read, on your system, and write down what I find, in order of what to fix first.

Durability and recovery

  1. Commit and replication settings, against what a lost write costs you
  2. Backups and point-in-time recovery, and whether a restore has been tested
  3. Failover: how it is triggered, and how long it takes

Performance

  1. The top queries by total time, with their plans
  2. Missing and unused indexes
  3. Bloat, vacuum or purge backlog, cache hit ratio, connection use

Cost

  1. Instance and storage size against the measured load
  2. Replicas, I/O, backups and non-production copies
  3. A safe order for cutting, as on the cost page

I work with PostgreSQL and MySQL, self-managed or on AWS, including Aurora and RDS.

The one invariant: COMMIT means the log record is on disk. pg_wal in PostgreSQL, #innodb_redo/#ib_redo0 + mysql-bin in MySQL, journal/WiredTigerLog + the oplog in MongoDB. The data files (base/…, *.ibd, collection-*.wt) are where checkpoints place the result afterwards, on their own schedule. Every guarantee on this page, durability, replication, point-in-time recovery, is a consequence of that ordering, and every number on this page is checkable with the commands given beside it.

PostgreSQL, MySQL/InnoDB, and MongoDB/WiredTiger internals, from physical layout to production failure modes. File names, paths, thresholds, and commands are the production ones.
