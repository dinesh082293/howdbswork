What's new

I add a line here when the site changes: new pages, new experiments, new tools. Newest first. Small fixes do not get a line; mistakes go on Corrections. The same list is an RSS feed.

  • Nov 19, 2026: the Query coach. Paste a PostgreSQL or MySQL query, and optionally the table definitions and an EXPLAIN plan, and get what I would flag in a review: risky statements, slow shapes, missing indexes, locking migrations and plan red flags, each with the fix and the lesson behind it. It runs in your browser, and the same checks are now a tool in the MCP server.
  • Nov 19, 2026: databases for AI agents. A new design doc, Agent memory and state, says where an agent's run log, checkpoints, long-term memories and working memory go. Notebook 8 measures pgvector on 100,000 memories: what they cost to store, recall against speed, and how a tenant filter returned 1.3 rows out of 10 until iterative scan was on. The site can now be used from Claude, Cursor or any MCP client: Use it from your AI assistant. And an agents review on Work with me.
  • Nov 19, 2026: a Your turn question at the end of twelve more lessons: the three engine pages, Compare, Ten concepts, the failover and cost decisions, anti-patterns, the capacity calculator, migrations, Cloud and the lab notebooks. Each one is a situation from real systems, with my answer folded until you ask for it.
  • Nov 19, 2026: Scale it is now on all 13 design docs, each following its own growth path (payments shard by account, bookings add a waiting room, telemetry puts a queue in front, and so on). Also a new Follow it on Lost updates and write skew: two sessions update the same row in PostgreSQL and InnoDB, with what REPEATABLE READ changes in PostgreSQL and what one forgotten transaction does in MySQL.
  • Nov 19, 2026: Scale it on the store, social feed and chat design docs: pick a stage of the design, from one database to sharded, then send ten times the traffic, empty the cache or fail the primary, and see what happens to latency, CPU and writes. The numbers are a model to show the shape; the measured ones are in the lab notebooks.
  • Nov 18, 2026: the site is now a course: every page except About has a lesson number, the home page keeps your place and shows the next lesson, and All pages lists all 42 lessons in order. Also new: Follow it, where you step through one read on the PostgreSQL and MySQL pages and one change travelling to a replica on Compare and MongoDB; and Estimate it on all 13 design docs, with sliders for your own numbers, a link that keeps them, and a Your turn question to answer before you open mine.
  • Nov 18, 2026: reading aids: On call tonight, three branching incidents where you pick what to check and learn why it was a good or bad call; a Check yourself quiz on nine pages; hover cards for database terms; a Contents button on long pages; links on headings; a reading progress bar; and a tick next to every page you have finished, kept in your browser.
  • Nov 18, 2026: one structure for the whole site: seven sections in reading order (Learn, Engines, Practice, Design, Get it right, Run it, About), a menu per section, a breadcrumb on every page, and at the bottom of every page its section's pages with previous and next links, so you can read the site from the first page to the last. Plus an All pages page, and fixes so every page fits a phone screen.
  • Nov 18, 2026: an animated commit on Compare (step through one change from memory to the log to the data file, for each engine), a problem and fix picture on every anti-pattern, a phase diagram for each migration playbook, and a "where your bill goes" chart on the cost page that takes your own invoice lines.

A picker, a who-does-what table, a full-cost comparison that counts people as well as the bill, the storage choices that matter on EC2, the honest version of databases on Kubernetes, and a "where to run it" section on every design doc.
