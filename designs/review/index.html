Designs · review

Submit a design for review

Is my database design sound?

If you are designing the data side of an application, send it in. I read what comes in and reply to the ones I can, with what I would change and why. Use the same outline as the design doc template: the workload first, then what goes where.

Two ways to send it

In public, or in private

In public, on GitHub

Open a design review issue in this site's repository. The form asks for the workload, the cost of a lost write, and your proposed placement. Other readers can learn from the review, and good ones may become a design doc on this site.

Open a design review issue

Public. Leave out company names, hostnames, credentials and customer data.

In private

For a design you cannot share, message me on LinkedIn with the engine, the rough size, and the one decision you are unsure about. For a full review of a running system, see Work with me.

What I look at

The questions every review asks

  1. Is there one source of truth for each kind of data?And is every other copy rebuildable from it?
  2. Which rules does the database enforce?Uniqueness, money, bookings, tenant isolation.
  3. What does a lost write cost, and do the commit and replication settings match?See what a commit waits for.
  4. Do the indexes match the three most frequent queries?See queries that look fine and are not.
  5. What happens at ten times today's load, and what breaks first?See the capacity calculator and the anti-patterns.

The one invariant: COMMIT means the log record is on disk. pg_wal in PostgreSQL, #innodb_redo/#ib_redo0 + mysql-bin in MySQL, journal/WiredTigerLog + the oplog in MongoDB. The data files (base/…, *.ibd, collection-*.wt) are where checkpoints place the result afterwards, on their own schedule. Every guarantee on this page, durability, replication, point-in-time recovery, is a consequence of that ordering, and every number on this page is checkable with the commands given beside it.

PostgreSQL, MySQL/InnoDB, and MongoDB/WiredTiger internals, from physical layout to production failure modes. File names, paths, thresholds, and commands are the production ones.
