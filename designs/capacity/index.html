Designs · capacity

Capacity calculator

How much memory, disk and log volume will this table need in a year?

You will learn: a first estimate of data size, working set, memory, disk and log volume, with every assumption written next to the number.

This is the arithmetic I do on a whiteboard before sizing a server, turned into a form. It gives a starting estimate, not a guarantee. Load a sample of real data and measure before you buy anything.

The assumptionsTable size is rows times row size plus 25 percent for page headers and free space. Each index, including the primary key, takes rows times entry size plus 40 percent for B-tree overhead. With a retention period, the row count levels off at new rows per day times retention days. Memory is the working set (the hot share of tables and indexes) plus 30 percent, plus 2 GB for the operating system, plus about 10 MB per connection. Disk keeps 30 percent free and adds 30 percent for logs, temporary files and growth surprises. Log volume is about 1.5 times the bytes changed per write. These are round numbers for a first estimate. Measure on real data.

The one invariant: COMMIT means the log record is on disk. pg_wal in PostgreSQL, #innodb_redo/#ib_redo0 + mysql-bin in MySQL, journal/WiredTigerLog + the oplog in MongoDB. The data files (base/…, *.ibd, collection-*.wt) are where checkpoints place the result afterwards, on their own schedule. Every guarantee on this page, durability, replication, point-in-time recovery, is a consequence of that ordering, and every number on this page is checkable with the commands given beside it.
