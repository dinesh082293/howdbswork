Analytics, dashboards and reporting

Where should reports and dashboards run, so they do not slow down the application?

Applications like this: business dashboards, finance reports, product analytics. This page is written the way I write a database design doc: the workload first, then where each kind of data goes and why, then how it grows, what breaks, and what I rejected.

The short answerNot on the production primary. Copy data to a column store (ClickHouse, BigQuery, Redshift, Snowflake, DuckDB for small cases) through change data capture or batch loads, and run reports there.

1 · The workload

What the application asks of the database

DimensionThis application
WritesBatch loads or a stream of changes from production.
ReadsBig scans with aggregates over a few columns.
Read to writeReads are few but each one reads a lot.
ConsistencyMinutes behind production is usually fine.
LatencySeconds for a dashboard, minutes for a heavy report.
GrowthLarge. All history, often from several systems.
2 · What goes where

Each kind of data, its store, and why

DataWhere it goesWhy
Fact tables (orders, events)A column storeReads only the columns a query names, compressed, in parallel.

The pipelineChange data capture (Debezium, or the managed equivalent) or scheduled batch exportsShips changes without running scans on the primary.

3 · Data model

The core tables and the queries they serve

-- ClickHouse
CREATE TABLE order_lines (
  order_id    UInt64,
  created_at  DateTime,
  customer_id UInt64,
  sku         LowCardinality(String),
  qty         UInt32,
  price_cents UInt64
) ENGINE = MergeTree
PARTITION BY toYYYYMM(created_at)
ORDER BY (sku, created_at);

SELECT sku, sum(qty * price_cents) / 100 AS revenue
FROM order_lines
WHERE created_at >= now() - INTERVAL 30 DAY
GROUP BY sku ORDER BY revenue DESC LIMIT 20;
  • The sort key is chosen for the most common filter, here sku and time.
  • Data arrives in batches. Column stores want thousands of rows per insert.
  • Reports read from the copy. The primary only pays for shipping the changes.
4 · How it grows

Start simple, change one thing at a time

  1. A read replica for reportsFine for small data. Long reports can conflict with replay on a PostgreSQL standby.
  2. Reports scan millions of rowsA column store fed by CDC or nightly loads.
  3. Many sourcesA warehouse or lakehouse with a modeling layer, and dashboards on top.
5 · What breaks first

The failures to plan for

What happensWhat you seeWhat I do
Reports on the primaryThe application slows down at report timeMove them. This is the most common cause of mystery slowdowns on a schedule.
Single-row inserts into a column storeToo many small parts, merges fall behindBatch the inserts.
Replica used for both failover and heavy reportsA long query delays replay, and the failover target is behindSeparate the two roles.
6 · Alternatives

What I considered and did not pick

  • More indexes on the primary for reports. Every index slows every write, and the scans still compete for cache.
  • Exporting CSV files by hand. Works once, then becomes a job nobody owns.

Cost. Warehouses bill by data scanned or by compute time. Partition and sort keys that match the common filters are the main lever.
